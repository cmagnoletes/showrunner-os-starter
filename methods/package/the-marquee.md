# The Marquee — what earns the click

The packaging doctrine of this Showrunner OS. The Marquee method
(`marquee-prompt.md`) and the `marquee` skill apply this file to every video you
package, and you should read it once yourself: it is how you judge any thumbnail the
system hands you, and any thumbnail you build with your own hands.

The one law: the title and the thumbnail are two shots at the same viewer. Spend them
on different things. The title names the stakes. The thumbnail shows the state, or asks
a question the title doesn't close. A thumbnail that repeats the title wastes a shot.

The title is locked before this method runs. Session 2 chose it with evidence, and the
video was written and recorded to keep its promise. The Marquee never rewrites the
title; it builds the other half of the package.

## Part 1 · Two shots, one promise

- Cover the title and read only the thumbnail. Write down the question it puts in a
  stranger's mind. Now read the title. It must answer a different question, or add
  stakes the thumbnail doesn't show. If both say the same thing, one of them is wasted.
- Together they must pose a question the viewer can only answer by clicking. Title as
  the question, thumbnail as the jeopardy, or thumbnail as a blunt answer with the why
  withheld.
- Use different triggers on each: curiosity on one, stakes on the other. The same
  emotion twice is one shot, not two.
- Canonical example: title "The four lessons I learned trying to make a million
  dollars"; thumbnail text "don't sell anything". The viewer is dropped inside an
  incomplete story and must click for lessons one to three.

## Part 2 · The words (a writing job, not a design job)

- Two to five words. Short words. If a viewer can't read it in the half second a feed
  gives them, it's too long.
- Never the title's words. Never the topic restated. The words add a dimension the
  photo can't carry: a stake, a number, a confession, a verdict, the thing they'd argue
  with.
- Open a loop. A complete sentence closes it. "I'm done" opens; "I'm done making
  videos" closes.
- Default to few; earn every word. If removing the text changes nothing about what the
  viewer understands, remove it.
- True to the video. The words point at something the beat sheet actually delivers, in
  the first minute if possible. High clicks plus early exits is the clickbait penalty:
  YouTube ranks on watch time share, not on the click.
- Three lenses to generate from, and to judge by:
  - **Curiosity:** what question does it raise that the title leaves open?
  - **Impact:** what is the stake, the cost, the number, the consequence?
  - **Controversy:** what claim would the viewer argue with, or assume is wrong?

## Part 3 · The photo (one real face)

- One photo of you. Real, well lit, lightly treated. Your face is the trust signal; a
  generated face is identifiable and blends into the feed.
- Paused in action beats posed shock. A closed-mouth worry, a focused look, a real
  smile mid-thought. Faces appear in 69 to 80 percent of breakout thumbnails; only 5
  percent use an exaggerated expression. Honest and legible beats staged.
- The expression matches the video's tone. Shock on a calm tutorial reads as bait and
  costs you the watch.
- Shoot right after you record: phone at eye level, window light in front of you, a
  plain wall behind you, five expressions in a minute. Keep props and furniture out of
  the frame; cutout tools keep whatever touches you.

## Part 4 · The frame (simplicity as the rule)

- Three elements at most: the face, one object if the archetype calls for one, the
  words. Empty space is a feature.
- The face fills 40 to 60 percent of the frame, separated hard from the background
  (blur, solid color, or a cutout on your brand color).
- Words in the left two-thirds, upper or lower band, clear of the face. The
  bottom-right corner belongs to YouTube's timestamp; keep it empty.
- Bold sans-serif, your brand font, high contrast against the background. One accent
  color, spent on the one word that carries the payoff, nowhere else.
- Judge it at 168 pixels wide (the sidebar) and at 120 (the phone), never at full size.
  If the words or the face fall apart small, they fail.
- Judge it in the feed, next to other thumbnails, never alone on a white canvas.

## Part 5 · The gate (grade before you upload)

Every thumbnail is scored on eleven criteria, 55 points, and must pass six tests before
it ships: the one-second glance, the postage stamp, the squint, the three-element count,
the title split, and the clickbait check. 42 or more with no failed test ships. Under 42
gets one targeted fix and a rescore. The rubric lives in
`.claude/skills/marquee/references/rubric.md`; the skill runs it on your exported image.
