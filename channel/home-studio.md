# The home studio — look and sound credible with what you already own

The recording setup for this channel. Most studio guides are written by gear obsessives,
for gear obsessives. This one gets you on camera fast, with the least equipment you can
get away with. Read it once, set up once. After that it's the five-minute checklist at
the bottom.

## The setup you can run today

Before buying a single thing, you can shoot a clean video this afternoon:

- [ ] Stand your phone on something solid at eye level. Back camera, never the selfie
      camera; it's sharper.
- [ ] Sit facing a window, with the window in front of you, never behind. Free,
      flattering light.
- [ ] Clip on a wireless mic (a DJI Mic or similar), about a hand's width below your
      chin. This one step matters more than any camera upgrade.
- [ ] Put three to six feet between you and the wall behind you. Depth alone makes the
      shot look intentional.
- [ ] Pick a quiet room with soft things in it (rugs, a couch, curtains). Hard empty
      rooms echo.
- [ ] Record a 15-second test and watch it back with the sound on. Fix anything obvious,
      then record for real.

If today is all you ever do, you'll already be ahead of most channels in your space.

## The background

Your background does quiet work: it tells people you're a professional before you say a
word. Leave space behind you (three to six feet blurs naturally, even on a phone). Show
one or two things that signal who you are: a shelf with books, a plant, a single framed
piece. Keep it simple and a touch darker than you are, so you stand out from it.

Avoid: sitting right up against a blank wall (reads flat and amateur), a window behind
you (the camera exposes for the window and turns you into a shadow), and clutter (eyes
wander to everything in the frame).

## Light

One good light in the right place does more than three lights in the wrong place.

- **Key light:** your main light. In front of you and off to one side, about 45 degrees,
  a little above eye level so it angles down on you.
- **Fill:** something softer on the other side to lift the shadows. A window or a white
  wall does this for free.
- **Backlight (optional):** a small light behind you, pointing at the wall, to separate
  you from the background.
- Diffused beats a bare bulb, and never light yourself from the ceiling; it casts
  shadows under your eyes. Keep every light the same color (all daylight or all warm),
  never mixed.

Start with just the key light.

## Phone settings (lock these once)

1. Back camera (the main 1x lens), recording 4K at 30fps.
2. Lock focus and exposure: in the Camera app, press and hold on your face until you see
   AE/AF Lock. The picture won't drift while you talk.
3. Frame your eyes about a third of the way down the screen, a little room above your
   head.
4. Airplane Mode and Do Not Disturb on, so a call never ruins a take. Check your
   storage; 4K eats space.
5. Wipe the lens. The cheapest upgrade there is.

Want a more cinematic image with manual control, still free: the Blackmagic Camera app.

## Sound

People forgive a soft-focus picture. They click away from bad audio in seconds.

- Mic about a hand's width below your chin, centered on your chest.
- Windscreen on if the room echoes or a fan is running.
- Quick level check: talk normally and make sure it isn't peaking into the red.
- If your mic records a backup onto itself, leave that on. Free insurance against a bad
  file.
- If you clap and the room rings, the mic hears that too. Soft things soak up echo; a
  couple of moving blankets hung behind and beside you is the cheap fix for a bare room.

## Recording a remote guest

Never trust the call itself for your recording. Each person records their own clean
track locally, then the tracks get stitched together. Zencastr does this free: create a
recording, send your guest the invite link (they join in the browser, nothing to
install), both of you wear headphones (earbuds are fine), hit record. It captures each
of you on a separate track in full quality, even if the internet hiccups. On a Mac, use
your iPhone as the webcam with Continuity Camera; on Windows, the Camo app does the same.
Keep your clip-on mic on for your own audio, exactly like a solo video.

## The shopping list (all of it optional)

Ballpark prices as of mid-2026. The budget starter kit, if you want one:

- A phone tripod with mount and remote, about $25. Tall enough to stand or sit at.
- A slim two-panel light kit (a key and a fill on small stands), about $100. A wand
  light works for a tight corner.
- Moving blankets, about $30, only if your room echoes.

Step-up, only when the channel is rolling: an app-controlled key light (about $90), a
small color tube behind you for separation (about $80), a desk mic like the Shure MV7
(about $200) for a richer broadcast sound on sitting-down videos. A real camera (a Sony
ZV-E10 II, a DJI Osmo Pocket 3) only when you want one. Your phone is genuinely fine
until then.

## The five-minute checklist (before every shoot)

The first setup takes about an hour. The second takes fifteen minutes. Once it's dialed
in, this is the routine before you hit record:

- [ ] Phone at eye level, back camera, 4K, lens wiped.
- [ ] Airplane Mode and Do Not Disturb on. Storage free.
- [ ] Mic clipped on, charged, levels checked.
- [ ] Key light on. Window light helping you, never behind you.
- [ ] Background tidy, with a little depth behind you.
- [ ] Record 15 seconds, watch it back with sound, then go.

The goal is a recorded video you're happy with, not a perfect one. Then hit record.
